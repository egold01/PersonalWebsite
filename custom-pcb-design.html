3.3, 5, 12V Voltage Supply PCB

Image placeholder: finished PCB, top layer
Image placeholder: finished PCB, bottom layer

In my Electronics & Controls class (junior year) I learned how to design PCBs (printed circuit boards) using the KiCAD software. The PCB I have pictured above takes an input of 12V from a barrel jack and then runs that power directly to a screw terminal to give a 12V output to power a DC motor and also runs the power through two voltage regulators to two series of pins - one that outputs 3.3V and one that outputs 5V. The pins are interfaceable with a breadboard's power rails so that one of the power rails can have a constant 3.3V and the other can have a constant 5V.

The other cool thing about this PBC design is that it has two layers - the top layer (on the left in the image above) is all of the "charged" wiring and the bottom layer (on the right in the image above) is all of the ground wiring.
